What it means

Countries began their decline at incomes from $1,100 to $24,000 a person: no wealth threshold sets the clock. Schooling was more alike but did not predict the year either. These are patterns, not causes.

Leave-one-out thresholds · paired Wilcoxon tests · bootstrap CI (5,000 resamples) · country-level association, not causation

Next we asked what countries looked like at their turning point. We linked 66 countries that turned between 1952 and 2013 to Maddison income data and Barro–Lee schooling of women aged 25 to 34. Income varied hugely: Bangladesh turned at about 1,100 dollars per person, Saudi Arabia at 24,000. Schooling was more similar: scaled by each measure’s world spread, it is 1.34 times more concentrated, with a bootstrap interval from 1.09 to 1.67. Then the honest test: using the typical level as a threshold to predict the turning-point year. Income misses by 14 years, worse than simply guessing the typical year. Schooling misses by 9.5 years, but so does the calendar. So money does not set the clock. Schooling is a steadier marker, but not a timer.
