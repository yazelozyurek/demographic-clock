What it means

The world is moving to small families, but not in step: in 2023 half the countries were below replacement while some still averaged six children. The fair question is when each country changes.

UN World Population Prospects 2024 · 1950–2023 estimates; dashed = UN projections · every excluded place is listed by name (excluded_places.csv)

In 2023, women in the Republic of Korea had 0.72 children on average; women in Niger had 6.06. In 1950, only 3 of 200 countries were below replacement; by 2023 it was 103. So we asked: do countries follow the same path, and when they reach the turning point, are they rich, or are their young women in school? Our data is UN World Population Prospects 2024. Summing every row of the file gives 363 billion people, because regions repeat countries; countries alone give the correct 8.09 billion. We keep the 200 countries above 100,000 people, 99.98 percent of the world, and list every place we leave out. Values after 2023 are UN projections, always drawn dashed.
