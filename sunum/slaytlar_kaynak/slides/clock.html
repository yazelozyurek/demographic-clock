What it means

Countries differ mainly in when they turn, not how: given its own clock, one curve fits 94%, and regions differ clearly in timing (p < 0.001). A later clock is not a worse country.

After SITAR growth curves (Cole et al., 2010) · * 16 of 200 separate fits hit their limits · region tests: 115 well-observed countries

Our first try fitted an S-shaped curve to each country separately. It fit, but gave implausible estimates: for Japan, whose decline happened before 1950, it said women started at nine children. So we borrowed the SITAR idea, SuperImposition by Translation And Rotation, from children’s growth charts: one shared S-curve, and one number per country — its turning point, the year fertility falls fastest. Watch what happens when we shift each country. By calendar year, time explains 27 percent of the variation in fertility. Shifted to their own turning points, one curve explains 94 percent, and it predicted best in backtests. On the map, the clock runs from Europe to Africa. In 2023, Nigeria is at its turning point, Türkiye passed it about forty years ago, Japan about ninety.
